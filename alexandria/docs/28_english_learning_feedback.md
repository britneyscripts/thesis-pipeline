## 📅 Log Entry: July 31, 2026 (Batch 11 - Essay Review)

### 22. Preposition with "Speak/Talk English"
- **Original:** *"I barely talked in English with someone."*
- **Improved:** *"I barely **spoke English with anyone**."* or *"I barely **talked to anyone in English**."*
- **Grammar Tips:**
  - Verb choice: **"Speak English"** is more common and formal than *"talk in English"*.
  - Negative compatibility: In a negative sentence (*"barely"*), use **anyone** instead of *someone*.
  - Preposition: We speak a language **with** someone, or talk **to** someone.

### 23. Adverb Comparison ("more deeply")
- **Original:** *"remember deeper why"*
- **Improved:** *"remember **more deeply** why..."*
- **Grammar Tips:**
  - Adverb modification: Use the comparative adverb **more deeply** to modify the verb *remember* (rather than the adjective *deeper*).

### 24. Preposition with "Seek" (Transitive Verb)
- **Original:** *"seeking for a job"*
- **Improved:** *"seeking **[ ]** a job"*
- **Grammar Tips:**
  - Transitive rule: The verb **seek** is transitive and takes its direct object immediately with NO preposition (*seeking a job*, not *seeking for*). If you want to use a preposition, use **looking for**.

### 25. Singular vs. Plural Idiom ("Behind the scenes")
- **Original:** *"what happens behind the scene"*
- **Improved:** *"what happens **behind the scenes**"*
- **Grammar Tips:**
  - Fixed idiom: The common C2 idiomatic phrase meaning "in secret/background" is always plural: **behind the scenes**.

### 26. Comparative Phrase ("as... as...")
- **Original:** *"probably they aren't so catastrophic like us"*
- **Improved:** *"probably they aren't **as catastrophic as we are**."*
- **Grammar Tips:**
  - Comparison construction: Use the **as [adjective] as [subject]** pattern for comparisons of equivalence/non-equivalence (*as catastrophic as we are*, not *so... like*).

## 📅 Log Entry: July 31, 2026 (Batch 10)

### 21. "Is it worth..." + Gerund (`-ing`)
- **Original:** *"is this worth read gemini 2.5 paper?"*
- **Improved:** *"**Is it worth reading the** Gemini 2.5 paper?"*
- **Grammar Tips:**
  - Standard phrase: Use **"Is it worth..."** (rather than *"is this worth..."*).
  - Verb pattern: The expression *"worth"* MUST be followed by a gerund: **worth reading** (never base verb *"worth read"*).
  - Definite article: Include **the** before specific proper nouns: *the Gemini 2.5 paper*.

## 📅 Log Entry: July 31, 2026 (Batch 9)

### 20. Question Formation & Preposition Match ("Did Google write a paper... for...")
- **Original:** *"Google wrote a paper dedicated to gemini 3.1 as they wrote to Gemini 1.5 and 2.5?"*
- **Improved:** *"**Did Google write** a dedicated paper for Gemini 3.1, **just like they did for** Gemini 1.5 and 2.5?"*
- **Grammar Tips:**
  - Question Inversion: In English, formal yes/no questions start with an auxiliary verb: **"Did Google write...?"** (rather than statement order with a question mark).
  - Comparative Clause: Use **"just as they did for..."** or **"just like they wrote for..."** (instead of *"as they wrote to"*).
  - Preposition: We write a paper **for** a model/topic or dedicated **to** a topic (*a paper dedicated to Gemini 3.1* or *a paper for Gemini 3.1*).

## 📅 Log Entry: July 28, 2026 (Batch 8)

### 18. Dummy "It" Subject & Infinitive ("It's better to apply...")
- **Original:** *"Well, maybe is better apply separation of concerns here."*
- **Improved:** *"Well, maybe **it is better to apply** separation of concerns here."*
- **Grammar Tip:** In English, sentences need an explicit subject (*it is better*, *it is necessary*). After adjectives like *better*, use the infinitive (*to apply*).

### 19. Question Word Order & Singular Agreement ("has", "for example")
- **Original:** *"could I use [] or (()) to find a paper that have this information about dualhybrid retriever for exemple? or this is something I need to do after we implement this rag system?"*
- **Improved:** *"Could I use `[[...]]` or `((...))` to find a paper that **has** this information about DualHybridRetriever, for **example**? Or **is this** something I need to do after we implement this RAG system?"*
- **Grammar Tips:**
  - Singular agreement: *a paper that **has*** (not *have*).
  - Spelling: **example** (with 'a', not 'e').
  - Question word order: Invert verb and subject in questions (*Or **is this** something...* instead of *"or this is..."*).

## 📅 Log Entry: July 28, 2026 (Batch 6)

### 15. Asking Opinions & Superlative Forms ("What do you think is the best way...")
- **Original:** *"How, you think, is the better way to store this information?"*
- **Improved:** *"What do you think is **the best way** to store this information?"*
- **Grammar Tips:**
  - Asking opinion: **"What do you think is..."** (not *"How, you think, is..."*).
  - Comparing 3+ options or ideal choice: Use superlative **the best way** (not *"the better way"*, which compares only 2 items).

### 16. Past Tense for Previous Messages & Spelling ("connect")
- **Original:** *"you write about... I need to conect the issue with this"*
- **Improved:** *"you **wrote** about... I need to **connect** the issue **to** this"*
- **Grammar Tips:**
  - Past message: Use past simple **you wrote about**.
  - Verb spelling: **connect** (double 'n').
  - Preposition: **connect [A] to [B]** (or *link [A] with [B]*).

- **Improved:** *"I'm seeing some tasks without **a version**, is this correct?"*

- **Original:** *"and I question about my api key I asked for permission to run a command here and I saw my api key.... here.... in the command"*

### 7. Present Understanding vs Past Simple
- **Original:** *"aaah I understood now."*
- **Improved:** *"Ah, I understand now!"* or *"Ah, now I get it!"*
- **Grammar Tip:** When a realization happens in the present moment ("now"), use the present simple (*"I understand now"*), not past simple (*"I understood"*).

### 8. Plural Nouns and Capitalization for Tools
- **Original:** *"You didn't update the task in linear."*
- **Improved:** *"You didn't update the tasks in Linear."*
- **Grammar Tip:** Use plural when referring to multiple tasks (*tasks*). Always capitalize proper product names (*Linear*).

### 9. Technical Terms Spelling
- **Original:** *"because you have the apy key in the env file."*
- **Improved:** *"because you have the API key in the .env file."*
- **Grammar Tip:** API is an acronym (Application Programming Interface), spelled **API**. File names starting with a dot like `.env` are usually written with the leading dot (*.env file*).

### 10. Subject-Verb Agreement with Plural Nouns
- **Original:** *"Did you create any new tasks that isn't there?"*
- **Improved:** *"Did you create any new tasks that **aren't** there?"*
- **Grammar Tip:** The relative clause refers to *tasks* (plural), so the verb must be plural: *tasks that aren't there* (not *isn't*).

## 📅 Log Entry: July 28, 2026 (Batch 1)

### 1. Announcing your return
- **Original:** *"I come back."*
- **Improved:** *"I'm back!"* or *"I've returned."*
- **Grammar Tip:** In English, "I come back" states a routine or general habit. When announcing that you have re-joined a conversation, use *"I'm back!"*.

### 2. Verb structure after "pleasure"
- **Original:** *"it's a pleasure work with you on this project."*
- **Improved:** *"It's a pleasure **working** with you on this project."* or *"It's a pleasure **to work** with you on this project."*
- **Grammar Tip:** The phrase *"It's a pleasure..."* is followed by either a gerund (*working*) or an infinitive (*to work*), never a base verb alone (*work*).

### 3. Subject-Verb pattern with "need"
- **Original:** *"I need that you add the due date..."*
- **Improved:** *"I need **you to add** the due date..."*
- **Grammar Tip:** Unlike Portuguese (*"Preciso que você adicione"*) or Spanish (*"Necesito que agregues"*), the English verb **need** follows the pattern **`Subject + need + Person + to + Verb`** (*I need you to do this*).

### 4. Preposition for "belong"
- **Original:** *"the version that each task belongs"*
- **Improved:** *"the version that each task belongs **to**"*
- **Grammar Tip:** The verb **belong** always takes the preposition **to** when referencing a group or category (*"Which version does this belong to?"*).

### 5. Prepositions require Gerunds (`-ing`)
- **Original:** *"without pollute this chat"*
- **Improved:** *"without **polluting** this chat"*
- **Grammar Tip:** Any verb coming directly after a preposition (like *without*, *by*, *for*, *after*, *before*) MUST take the `-ing` form.

### 6. Action verb choice ("put" vs "pull")
- **Original:** *"just pull all the mistake in the file."*
- **Improved:** *"Just **put** all the **mistakes** in the file."*
- **Grammar Tip:** Use **put** (colocar/inserir) rather than **pull** (puxar/arrastar). Also remember plural agreement: *all the mistakes*.
